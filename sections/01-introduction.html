Modern Artificial Intelligence systems rely on complex data pipelines where raw inputs are ingested from multiple heterogeneous sources and subjected to continuous transformation processes before use. Rather than being created in isolation, AI training and inference datasets are often dynamically derived from upstream datasets through multiple processing stages, forming complex dependency chains. While these data engineering workflows enable the development of increasingly sophisticated AI models, they also raise concerns regarding the origin of data, the transformations applied throughout the pipeline, the trustworthiness of different data sources, and the overall transparency of the resulting datasets.

This this work, we discuss how Explainable AI (XAI) techniques can be extended to incorporate data provenance information, enabling users to trace the lineage of individual data points back to their original sources and transformations.

Integrating data provenance into XAI unlocks critical real-world applications across high-stakes domains. It enables automated root-cause analysis when model performance degrades, allows auditors to verify regulatory compliance and data governance standards, and aids in detecting data poisoning attacks. Furthermore, fine-grained provenance facilitates targeted data attribution and machine unlearning, empowering organizations to remove corrupted or restricted data contributions without blindly retraining entire models from scratch.
