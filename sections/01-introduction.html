Modern Artificial Intelligence systems rely on complex data pipelines where raw inputs are ingested from multiple heterogeneous sources and pass through continuous transformation processes before use. Consequently, AI training and inference datasets are often dynamically derived from upstream datasets through multiple processing stages, forming complex dependency chains. While these data engineering workflows enable the development of increasingly sophisticated AI models, they also raise concerns regarding the origin of data, the transformations applied throughout the pipeline, the trustworthiness of different data sources and the overall transparency of the resulting datasets.

We discuss how data provenance can be leveraged to support end-to-end explainability across data analytics and machine learning (ML) pipelines. We introduce the core concepts of provenance semirings and semimodules to construct fine-grained algebraic annotations over relational operations and aggregate queries. By mapping the contribution of individual input elements into elementary conjunction, disjunction and aggregation metadata, these annotations enable the deterministic tracing of analytical and ML results back to their raw sources. This provides a formal and transparent baseline for debugging and explaining data-driven outcomes.

We present two examples demonstrating how data provenance supports data lineage and root-cause analysis in AI pipelines. The first traces output data back to its contributing sources, while the second identifies how upstream data preparation can influence employee promotion predictions and reveal potential fairness issues. Provenance polynomials capture these dependencies from both data and pipeline perspectives, complemented by graphical visualizations for improved interpretability. The approach is model-agnostic and independent of the predictive model used.
